## 3. Full description (≤4000 chars)

```
Unbound is a chain-reaction puzzle about escape.

Every node on the board points somewhere. Tap one and it fires along its arrow —
if the path out is clear, it leaves the board. If anything is in the way, it
does not. Clearing the board is never about speed or luck. It is about finding
the one order that lets every piece out.

There is no randomness in a solution. Every board you are given is solvable, and
every board can be reasoned about from the position in front of you.

FIND THE PATH. FREE THE BOARD.

• Tap a node to extract it along its arrow
• A blocked node stays put — read the board before you commit
• Three lives per level; a blocked tap is a foul
• Undo any move, or hold to restart the board
• Turn on the Aim Guide to preview a node's exit path before you tap

MECHANICS THAT STACK
Later stages layer systems on top of the base rule:
• Cores — priority nodes the level scores you on ("Cores: 2/5" in the HUD)
• Locked nodes — sealed until all four of their neighbours are gone
• Relays — extracting one rotates its whole row 90 degrees, redirecting
  everything still on it
• Phase gates — a group of nodes that cannot be touched until the preceding
  phase is fully cleared

A CAMPAIGN THAT KEEPS GOING
Three difficulty tracks — Easy, Medium and Hard — each with their own colour
temperature and their own pacing. The campaign runs into four digits, navigated
by a drill-down breadcrumb rather than an endless scroll. Earn up to three stars
per stage, chase directives like FLAWLESS, and watch your frontier number climb.

NETWORK INCIDENTS
A new daily challenge every day, laid out on a month calendar. Today is always
free. Missed a day? Earlier incidents can be replayed.

ACHIEVEMENTS
Progress syncs to Google Play Games achievements when you are signed in — and the
full catalogue stays browsable on-device whether you sign in or not.

BUILT TO BE READABLE
• High Contrast mode swaps in a colourblind-safe node palette
• Sound, haptics and background motion can each be turned off independently
• Every control carries a screen-reader label
• Ghost hints pulse a legal move if you stall

NO ACCOUNT. NO SIGN-UP.
Your progress lives on your device. Play offline. One purchase removes ads
forever.
```

Word choices to keep: "chain reaction", "deterministic", "no account".
Words to avoid: "endless", "levels generated by AI", "match-3" — none are true.

## 4. Target audience & content

### Age groups (Play Console → App content → Target audience)

Select **13-15, 16-17, 18 and over**.

**Do NOT select any under-13 band.** Ticking a band below 13 pulls the app into
the Play **Families** programme, which would require a Families-certified ads SDK
configuration, block the current AdMob setup as configured, and add a designed-for-
families content review. The app is not built for that: it serves AdMob ads with
UMP consent and syncs to Play Games, and neither is set up under Families policy.

Answer "Does your app appeal to children?" → **No**. Store assets must avoid
child-directed styling (cartoon mascots, primary-colour play patterns). The
current dark neon aesthetic is consistent with a 13+ answer.

### Ads declaration

**Contains ads: YES.** Verified placements (`lib/services/ads/ad_placements.dart`):

| Format | Placement |
| --- | --- |
| Rewarded | continue after lives run out (Hard campaign), extra undo, extra hint, unlock a past daily |
| Interstitial | between campaign levels, gated by a win-streak/frustration check |
| Banner | game screen, pause overlay, daily challenge calendar |

Ads are suppressed entirely for premium users (`PremiumAdServiceDecorator`).
`maxAdContentRating` is set to **T** (teen) in `lib/main.dart:126`, which matches the 13+ target-audience answer above.

## 6. Data safety

Answers derive from `docs/sdk_inventory.md`, which classifies each SDK as a
**service provider** (processing on our behalf → "collected, not shared") or a
**third party** (→ "shared"). Google Mobile Ads is a third party; everything else
in the stack is a service provider.

**Top-level answers**

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Does your app collect or share any of the required user data types? | **Yes** |
| Is all user data encrypted in transit? | **Yes** (all SDKs use HTTPS; the app makes no plaintext network calls of its own) |
| Do you provide a way for users to request that their data be deleted? | **Yes — users can request data deletion** (see §7) |
| Is any data collected required, or can users choose? | Mixed — see the table |

**Data types**

| Category → type | Collected | Shared | Purpose | Required? | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Device or other IDs → Device or other IDs | Yes | **Yes — Google Mobile Ads (third party)** | Advertising or marketing; Analytics | Required (unless premium, which suppresses ads) | `google_mobile_ads`, Firebase Analytics app-instance ID |
| Location → Approximate location | Yes | **Yes — Google Mobile Ads** | Advertising or marketing | Required | AdMob derives coarse geography from IP for ad serving. The app itself requests no location permission (both are `tools:node="remove"`d). Declared conservatively; confirm against Google's current AdMob Data Safety guidance before submitting. |
| App activity → App interactions | Yes | No (Firebase Analytics is a service provider) | Analytics | Required — no in-app analytics opt-out exists | `lib/services/analytics/firebase_analytics_service.dart` |
| App info and performance → Crash logs | Yes | No (Crashlytics is a service provider) | Analytics; App functionality | Required | `lib/bootstrap/firebase_bootstrap.dart` |
| App info and performance → Diagnostics | Yes | No | Analytics; App functionality | Required | same |
| Financial info → Purchase history | Yes | No (RevenueCat and Play Billing are service providers) | App functionality | Required for buyers only | `revenue_cat_subscription_service.dart` |
| Personal info → User IDs | Yes | No (Play Games Services) | App functionality | **Optional** — only if the player signs in to Google Play Games | `lib/services/achievements/play_games_auth.dart` |

**What is NOT collected:** name, email address, phone number, address, payment
card details (handled entirely inside Google Play's own checkout), contacts,
photos, files, messages, precise location, health data, audio, camera.

**Local-only data (not a Data Safety declaration, but state the truth in the
listing and policy):** stars, unlocked frontier per difficulty, selected
difficulty, tutorial completion, daily-challenge results, achievement counters
and a cached premium flag — all in a local Hive box
(`lib/services/storage/hive_chain_pop_persistence.dart`). None of it is uploaded
by the app.

> Under-declaring the AdMob "shared" answer is the single most common rejection
> cause for this SDK stack. Device IDs must be marked **shared**, not merely
> collected.

## 7. Deletion request route

Play requires an **account-deletion URL only for apps that let users create an
account**. Unbound has no account system: there is no sign-up, no login, no
server-side profile, and no user record keyed to an identity we control. The
account-deletion URL requirement therefore **does not apply**.

Answer Data Safety's deletion question with **"Users can request that data be
deleted"**, backed by a contact address published in the privacy policy.

Route to publish:

1. **In-app, immediate, local:** the **"Reset Game Data"** control (long-press the
   Play button on the main menu → destructive confirm dialog →
   `StorageLocator.instance.clearProgress()`). It clears the local Hive box and
   nothing else. It does **not** delete data already held by Google, Firebase,
   AdMob, RevenueCat or Play Games, and the premium entitlement is deliberately
   preserved across it (`clearProgress` re-writes `cachedPremium`).
2. **By email, for anything server-side:** a request to the support address
   published in `docs/privacy.md` (`adbkv.apps@gmail.com`, from
   `lib/config/chain_pop_legal.dart`). Requests are actioned by deleting the
   corresponding RevenueCat customer record and, where applicable, submitting a
   Firebase/AdMob deletion request.
3. **Platform controls:** Google account-level ad settings and the Play Games
   account/data controls sit with Google, not with us; the policy links out
   rather than promising deletion we cannot perform.

**OPEN ITEM:** confirm `adbkv.apps@gmail.com` is the address that should appear
publicly, that it is monitored, and that the same address is published in the
hosted privacy policy — Play cross-checks the two.

## 9. Countries and rollout strategy

### Rollout mechanics — read this first

**A first production release cannot use a staged percentage rollout.** Play only
offers percentage staged rollout from the *second* production release onward; the
first release goes to 100% of the selected countries at once. Consequently:

- **Country selection is the only risk lever available for release #1.**
- The plan is: launch broad but with a deliberately excluded set, then use staged
  percentage rollout from release #2 for anything riskier.
- Before production, run the closed test (12 testers / 14 days) — that, not the
  rollout slider, is the real safety net for release #1.

### Recommended selection

**Launch in all available countries EXCEPT the list below.** Each exclusion has a
written reason; a blanket "everywhere except China/Vietnam/Russia" with no
rationale is not a strategy and gives you nothing to revisit later.

| Excluded | Reason | Revisit when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mainland China | Google Play does not operate there; the app depends on Google Play Services, Play Billing, Play Games and AdMob, none of which are available. Selecting it achieves nothing. | Never, on this stack |
| Russia, Belarus | Google Play billing is suspended for these markets, so the ad-removal IAP cannot be sold; AdMob monetisation is effectively nil while support burden is not. | If Play billing is restored |
| Vietnam | Games distributed in Vietnam are subject to a local game-licensing regime that in practice requires a licensed local publisher. Excluding avoids shipping into a compliance obligation we have not met. | If a local publishing partner is arranged |
| South Korea | Korean game distribution runs through the GRAC rating route rather than plain IARC. Excluded pending confirmation that the IARC certificate covers Korea for this title. | After confirming the GRAC/IARC position — this is a check, not a permanent exclusion |
| Sanctioned territories (Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Syria, Crimea/Donetsk/Luhansk) | Play does not distribute there; not selectable in practice. | Never |

Everything else — including all of the EU/EEA and UK (covered by the UMP/GDPR
consent flow already implemented), the US (device IDs and approximate location are
declared, and `maxAdContentRating` is G), Canada, Australia, Japan, India, Brazil,
LatAm, SEA excluding Vietnam, and the rest of APAC — is in scope for release #1.

**Localisation:** the app ships English-only strings. That is acceptable for a
broad launch of a near-textless puzzle, but the *store listing* should at minimum
add localised listings for the largest non-English markets before pushing
acquisition spend. **OPEN ITEM** — decide which listing locales to translate.

**Note on the EU:** the app already shows the Google UMP consent form before
`MobileAds.instance.initialize()` and refuses to initialise ads if the consent
info never resolves (`lib/services/ads/ump_consent.dart`), and it exposes a
"Manage ad choices" re-consent entry point. That is what makes an EU launch
defensible on day one.
